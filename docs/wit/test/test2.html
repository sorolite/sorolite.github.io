The arc length formula is ∫t1t2‖𝐫′(t)‖dt\int_{t_1}^{t_2} \Vert \mathbf{r}^\prime (t) \Vert \mathrm{d}t. Here, that gives:

∫15‖𝐫′(t)‖dt=∫15‖⟨t6,3,t2⟩‖dt=∫15(t6)2+32+(t2)2dt=∫15t4+6t2+9dt=∫15(t2+3)dt=[13t3+3t]t=15=(1353+3(5))−(1313+3(1))=(1253+15)−(13+3)=1703−103=1603 \begin{aligned} \int_1^5 \left\Vert \mathbf{r}^\prime (t) \right\Vert \mathrm{d}t & = \int_1^5 \left\Vert \left\langle t\sqrt{6}, 3, t^2 \right\rangle \right\Vert \mathrm{d}t \\ & = \int_1^5 \sqrt{{\left(t\sqrt{6}\right)}^2 + 3^2 + {\left(t^2\right)}^2} \mathrm{d}t \\ & = \int_1^5 \sqrt{t^4 +6t^2 + 9} \mathrm{d} t \\ & = \int_1^5 \left( t^2 + 3 \right) \mathrm{d}t \\ & = {\left[ \tfrac{1}{3}t^3 + 3t \right]}_{t=1}^5 \\ & = \left(\tfrac{1}{3}5^3 + 3(5) \right) - \left(\tfrac{1}{3}1^3 + 3(1) \right) \\ & = \left(\tfrac{125}{3}+15\right) - \left(\tfrac{1}{3}+3\right) \\ & = \tfrac{170}{3} - \tfrac{10}{3} \\ & = \tfrac{160}{3} \end{aligned}

Problem 10

Find the directional derivative of f(x,y)=−4xy−14x4−14y4f(x,y) = -4xy -\tfrac{1}{4}x^4 - \tfrac{1}{4}y^4 at the point (1,−1)(1,-1) in the direction of the unit vector 𝐮=⟨122,−122⟩\mathbf{u} = \left\langle \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}, -\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{2} \right\rangle.

Reveal solution

The directional derivative is equal to the dot product of the gradient vector with the unit direction vector, D𝐮f=∇f⋅𝐮D_{\mathbf{u}} f = \nabla f \cdot \mathbf{u}. Therefore we must find the gradient vector:

∇f(x,y)=⟨fx(x,y),fy(x,y)⟩=⟨−4y−x3,−4x−y3⟩. \begin{aligned} \nabla f(x,y) & = \left\langle f_x(x,y), f_y(x,y) \right\rangle \\ & = \left\langle -4y-x^3, -4x-y^3 \right\rangle. \end{aligned}

Then evaluate it at the point (1,−1)(1,-1) to obtain

∇f(1,−1)=⟨−4(−1)−(1)3,−4(1)−(−1)3⟩=⟨4−1,−4+1⟩=⟨3,−3⟩. \begin{aligned} \nabla f(1,-1) & = \left\langle -4(-1)-(1)^3, -4(1)-(-1)^3 \right\rangle \\ & = \left\langle 4-1, -4+1 \right\rangle \\ & = \left\langle 3, -3 \right\rangle. \end{aligned}

The directional derivative is therefore

D𝐮f(1,−1)=∇f(1,−1)⋅𝐮=⟨3,−3⟩⋅⟨122,−122⟩=3(122)+(−3)(−122)=32. \begin{aligned} D_\mathbf{u} f (1,-1) & = \nabla f(1,-1) \cdot \mathbf{u} \\ & = \langle 3, -3 \rangle \cdot \left\langle \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}, -\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{2} \right\rangle \\ & = 3\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}\right) + (-3)\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}\right) \\ & = 3\sqrt{2}. \end{aligned}

Problem 14

Consider the surface xy+yz+zx=5xy + yz + zx = 5. Find the equation of the tangent plane to the surface at the point (1,2,1)(1,2,1).

Reveal solution

We say that f(x,y,z)=xy+yz+zx−5f(x,y,z) = xy + yz + zx. Then the equation for the tangent plane is given by ∇f(1,2,1)⋅⟨x−1,y−2,z−1⟩=0\nabla f (1,2,1) \cdot \langle x-1, y-2, z-1 \rangle = 0. We therefore first find the gradient vector:

∇f(x,y,z)=⟨fx,fy,fz⟩=⟨y+z,x+z,y+x⟩⇒∇f(1,2,1)=⟨2+1,1+1,2+1⟩=⟨3,2,3⟩. \begin{aligned} \nabla f(x,y,z) & = \left\langle f_x, f_y, f_z \right\rangle \\ & = \langle y+z, x+z, y+x \rangle \\ \Rightarrow \nabla f(1,2,1) & = \langle 2+1, 1+1, 2+1 \rangle \\ & = \langle 3, 2, 3 \rangle. \end{aligned}

Then the equation of the tangent plane is

∇f(1,2,1)⋅⟨x−1,y−2,z−1⟩=0⇒⟨3,2,3⟩⋅⟨x−1,y−2,z−1⟩=0⇒3(x−1)+2(y−2)+3(z−1)=0⇒3x−3+2y−4+3z−3=0⇒3x+2y+3z−10=0. \begin{aligned} \nabla f(1,2,1) \cdot \langle x-1, y-2, z-1 \rangle & = 0 \\ \Rightarrow \langle 3, 2, 3 \rangle \cdot \langle x-1, y-2, z-1 \rangle & = 0 \\ \Rightarrow 3(x-1) + 2(y-2) + 3(z-1) & = 0 \\ \Rightarrow 3x-3 + 2y-4 + 3z-3 & = 0 \\ \Rightarrow 3x + 2y + 3z -10 & = 0. \end{aligned}

Problem 20

Find all critical points of z=−x2+y2z = -x^2 + y^2. Perform the second-derivative test to classify each point (determine if the point is a local maximum, a local minimum, a saddle point) or state that the test is inconclusive.

Reveal solution

To find critical points, we find all first-order partial derivatives:

∂z∂x=−2x∂z∂y=2y \begin{aligned} \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} & = -2x \\ \frac{\partial z}{\partial y} & = 2y \end{aligned}

The only critical point will be the point where both partial derivatives are 00, i.e. at the point (0,0)(0,0).

The second-derivative test requires us to find the determinant of the Hessian matrix.

D=|H|=|∂2z∂x2∂2z∂x∂y∂2z∂y∂x∂2z∂y2|=(∂2z∂x2)(∂2z∂y2)−(∂2z∂y∂x)2=(−2)(2)−02=−4. \begin{aligned} D & = \left\vert H \right\vert \\ & = \left\vert \begin{array}{cc} \frac{\partial^2 z}{\partial x^2} & \frac{\partial^2 z}{\partial x \partial y} \\ \frac{\partial^2 z}{\partial y \partial x} & \frac{\partial^2 z}{\partial y^2} \end{array} \right\vert \\ & = \left(\frac{\partial^2 z}{\partial x^2}\right) \left(\frac{\partial^2 z}{\partial y^2} \right) - {\left( \frac{\partial^2 z}{\partial y \partial x}\right)}^2 \\ & = (-2)(2)-0^2 \\ & = -4. \end{aligned}

Strictly speaking, we should plug the coordinates of the critical point (0,0)(0,0) into the function D(x,y)D(x,y). But this is somewhat superfluous here, as in this case DD is a constant function that always returns the output −4-4. D(0,0)=−4<0D(0,0) = -4 < 0, therefore the critical point (0,0)(0,0) is a saddle point.

Problem 24

The quantity xyxy has a maximum possible value when it is subject to the constraint 10x+6y=84010x + 6y = 840. Find this value, and the (x,y)(x,y)-point at which it occurs.

Reveal solution

Identify the objective function f(x,y)f(x,y), the constraint function g(x,y)g(x,y), and set up the system of equations ∇f=λ∇g\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g.

We have f(x,y)=xyf(x,y)=xy and g(x,y)=10x+6y−840g(x,y) = 10x + 6y -840. Therefore ∇f=⟨y,x⟩\nabla f = \langle y, x \rangle and ∇g=⟨10,6⟩\nabla g = \langle 10, 6 \rangle; then our system of simultaneous equations is

y=λ(10)x=λ(6)10x+6y=840. \begin{aligned} y & = \lambda (10) \\ x & = \lambda (6) \\ 10x + 6y & = 840. \end{aligned}

We can solve this through rearrangement:

y10=λ=x6⇒6y=10x⇒10x+10x=840⇒x=42⇒y=70. \begin{aligned} \frac{y}{10} & = \lambda = \frac{x}{6} \\ \Rightarrow 6y & = 10x \\ \Rightarrow 10x + 10x & = 840 \\ \Rightarrow x & = 42 \\ \Rightarrow y & = 70. \end{aligned}

Therefore the maximum value of xyxy under the constraint 10x+6y=84010x+6y=840 is 42×70=294042\times 70 = 2940, at the point (x,y)=(42,70)(x,y)=(42,70).
