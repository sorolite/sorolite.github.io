Directional derivatives

Practice problems for directional derivatives.

Problem 1

Find ∇f\nabla f for f(x,y)=x2+3y2f(x,y)=x^2+3y^2 and evaluate it at (1,−1)(1,-1).

Reveal solution

The gradient ∇f\nabla f is the vector of first-order partial derivatives of ff.

The first-order partial derivatives are fx(x,y)=2xf_x (x,y) = 2x and fy(x,y)=6yf_y (x,y) = 6y. Therefore ∇f=⟨2x,6y⟩\nabla f = \langle 2x, 6y \rangle.

To evaluate it at (1,−1)(1,-1), we plug in the values x=1x=1 and y=−1y=-1. This gives ⟨2(1),6(−1)⟩=⟨2,−6⟩\langle 2(1), 6(-1) \rangle = \langle 2, -6 \rangle.

Problem 2

Find ∇f\nabla f for f(x,y)=xeyf(x,y)=xe^y and evaluate it at (2,0)(2,0).

Reveal solution

The gradient ∇f\nabla f is the vector of first-order partial derivatives of ff.

The first-order partial derivatives are fx(x,y)=eyf_x (x,y) = e^y and fy(x,y)=xeyf_y (x,y) = xe^y. Therefore ∇f=⟨ey,xey⟩\nabla f = \langle e^y, x e^y \rangle.

To evaluate it at (2,0)(2,0), we plug in the values x=2x=2 and y=0y=0. This gives ⟨e0,2e0⟩=⟨1,2⟩\langle e^0, 2e^0 \rangle = \langle 1, 2 \rangle.

Problem 3

Find ∇f\nabla f for f(x,y,z)=xy+z2f(x,y,z)=xy+z^2 and evaluate it at (1,2,−1)(1,2,-1).

Reveal solution

The gradient ∇f\nabla f is the vector of first-order partial derivatives of ff.

The first-order partial derivatives are fx(x,y,z)=yf_x (x,y,z) = y, fy(x,y,z)=xf_y (x,y,z) = x, and fz(x,y,z)=2zf_z (x,y,z) = 2z. Therefore ∇f=⟨y,x,2z⟩\nabla f = \langle y, x, 2z \rangle.

To evaluate it at (1,2,−1)(1,2,-1), we plug in the values x=1x=1, y=2y=2, and z=−1z=-1. This gives ⟨2,1,2(−1)⟩=⟨2,1,−2⟩\langle 2, 1, 2(-1) \rangle = \langle 2, 1, -2 \rangle.

Problem 4

Find the directional derivative of f(x,y)=x2+y2f(x,y)=x^2+y^2 at (1,2)(1,2) in direction ⟨3,4⟩\langle3,4\rangle.

Reveal solution

If we find a unit direction vector 𝐮\mathbf{u}, the directional derivative will be ∇f⋅𝐮\nabla f \cdot \mathbf{u}.

To get a unit direction vector, we divide the direction vector ⟨3,4⟩\langle 3, 4 \rangle by its own magnitude, which is 32+42=5\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5. Therefore our unit direction vector is 𝐮=⟨35,45⟩\mathbf{u} = \left\langle \tfrac{3}{5}, \tfrac{4}{5} \right\rangle.

The gradient vector ∇f\nabla f is the vector of first-order partial derivatives of ff, i.e., ∇f=⟨fx,fy⟩=⟨2x,2y⟩\nabla f = \left\langle f_x , f_y \right\rangle = \langle 2x, 2y \rangle.

We evaluate the gradient vector at the point (1,2)(1,2) by substituting in x=1x=1 and y=2y=2: ∇f(1,2)=⟨2(1),2(2)⟩=⟨2,4⟩\nabla f(1,2) = \langle 2(1), 2(2) \rangle = \langle 2, 4 \rangle.

The directional derivative is the dot product: for a unit direction vector 𝐮\mathbf{u}, we have:

D𝐮f=∇f⋅𝐮=⟨2,4⟩⋅⟨35,45⟩=235+445=65+165=225=4.4 \begin{align} D_{\mathbf{u}} f & = \nabla f \cdot \mathbf{u} \\ & = \langle 2, 4 \rangle \cdot \left\langle \frac{3}{5}, \frac{4}{5} \right\rangle \\ & = 2\frac{3}{5} + 4\frac{4}{5} \\ & = \frac{6}{5} + \frac{16}{5} \\ & = \frac{22}{5} = 4.4 \end{align}
