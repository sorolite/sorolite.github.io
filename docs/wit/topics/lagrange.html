Lagrange Multipliers

Practice problems for lagrange multipliers.

Problem 1

Use Lagrange multipliers to find the maximum and minimum of x+yx+y subject to x2+y2=2x^2+y^2=2.

Reveal solution

Our objective function is f(x,y)=x+yf(x,y) = x+y. Our constraint function is g(x,y)=x2+y2−2g(x,y) = x^2 + y^2 -2.

The gradient of ff is ∇f=⟨fx,fy⟩=⟨1,1⟩\nabla f = \left\langle f_x , f_y \right\rangle = \langle 1,1 \rangle. The gradient of gg is ∇g=⟨gx,gy⟩=⟨2x,2y⟩\nabla g = \left\langle g_x , g_y \right\rangle = \langle 2x, 2y \rangle.

∇f\nabla f and ∇g\nabla g are parallel vectors: there is a real number λ\lambda, the Lagrange multiplier, with the property that ∇f=λ∇g\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g, i.e., ⟨1,1⟩=λ⟨2x,2y⟩\langle 1, 1 \rangle = \lambda \langle 2x, 2y \rangle. Combining this with the constraint g(x,y)=0g(x,y) = 0, this gives us the set of three simultaneous equations:

1=2λx1=2λyx2+y2=2 \begin{align} 1 & = 2\lambda x \\ 1 & = 2\lambda y \\ x^2 + y^2 & = 2 \end{align}

We can rearrange the first equation 1=2λx1 = 2\lambda x to give λ=12x\lambda = \tfrac{1}{2x}. Then we can plug this into the second equation 1=2λy1 = 2\lambda y to get 1=212xy=yx1 = 2\tfrac{1}{2x} y = \tfrac{y}{x}.

Rearranging this result gives x=yx = y; we can plug this into the final equation to get x2+x2=2x^2 + x^2 = 2. Solving for xx, we obtain x2=1x^2 = 1 and therefore x=±1x=\pm 1.

Since x=yx=y, the extrema are therefore at the points (1,1)(1,1) and (−1,−1)(-1,-1). Evaluating the function at these points, we get f(1,1)=1+1=2f(1,1)=1+1=2 and f(−1,−1)=(−1)+(−1)=−2f(-1,-1)=(-1)+(-1)=-2.

Hence, on the curve x2+y2=2x^2 + y^2 = 2, f(x,y)=x+yf(x,y)=x+y has a maximum value of 22 at the point (1,1)(1,1) and a minimum value of −2-2 at the point (−1,−1)(-1,-1).
